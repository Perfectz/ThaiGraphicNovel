# Battle feedback audio

The battle now has eleven original, synthesized cues: strike, hurt, guard, parry, dodge, break, heal, expose, duet, victory and defeat. They are brief, pitched/filtered signals designed to distinguish combat outcomes without introducing speech or additional downloads. `battleCues.ts` produces deterministic mono samples; `BattleAudio.ts` caches the buffers inside one lazily created Web Audio context per mounted battle.

`useBattleAudio.ts` responds only to new battle event serials. Loading a saved battle or previewing a word art does not replay an event. A newly broken enemy gets the break cue even when advancing the turn immediately consumes its stagger. Victory and defeat take precedence over ordinary hit sounds.

The command panel has an independent, persisted **Battle sound on/off** control. Older saves with music muted default to muted effects until explicitly enabled. Music and effects can subsequently be controlled separately. Playback requires a user gesture; blocked cues are discarded, not queued. Thai practice audio focus, page hiding and muting stop current effects. Retiring the battle stops/disconnects sources, clears buffers, disconnects the output and closes the context. Visual battle feedback remains available without Web Audio.

The synthesis includes short attack/tail envelopes and fixed headroom. The preview uses the same samples and output gain as the runtime: `artifacts/battle-audio/cue-preview.wav`. `cue-manifest.json` identifies each cue's start and duration. Regenerate it with `node scripts/render-battle-cues.mjs`. These are original fantasy game effects, not recordings or reproductions of traditional Thai instruments.

Verification includes five focused tests for waveform validity/headroom, distinct deterministic output, outcome selection, old preference migration, gesture/focus/mute behavior, buffer reuse and disposal, plus fifteen combat tests. `scripts/test-battle-audio.mjs` exercises live desktop/phone playback, Thai reference playback without effects, muted outcomes, preference persistence, no old effect on reload, guard/break/heal outcomes and context closure on retreat. The final report is `artifacts/battle-audio/browser/progress.json`; both cases pass without unexpected page or console errors. The test instruments actual Web Audio starts rather than treating a requested cue as proof that audio started.

The full combat regression is `scripts/test-expedition.mjs`, with evidence under `artifacts/battle-audio/full-combat/`. It additionally observes cues during actual parry, dodge, healing, damage, victory and defeat flows. Automated sample/playback checks and desktop phone emulation do not establish subjective sound quality, headphone comfort, physical-phone behavior or Thai pronunciation quality.

Final result: the full combat regression exited successfully with `finished: true` and no page or console errors. Production build, focused ESLint, all twenty audio/settings/combat tests and ten Whisper tests passed; both Vite-proxied voice health endpoints returned HTTP 200. Desktop and phone control layouts were visually inspected. No combat damage, rewards, phrase content or speech-assessment rules changed.
